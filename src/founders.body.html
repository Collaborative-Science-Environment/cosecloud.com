About CoSE

A small company that builds research instruments everyone can use.

Collaborative Science Environment Inc. is a Wisconsin benefit corporation based in Madison. We design, print, assemble and test every SciSpinner ourselves, publish the control software under the GPL, and ship each unit with the same documentation our engineers work from.

Founders, past and present

Co-founder · CEO

Dr. Richard Barker

Richard is an astrobotanist and senior scientist at Purdue University, where he studies how plants respond to microgravity and designs bioregenerative life-support systems for habitats beyond Earth. A member of the NASA Lunar LEAF team, he holds a doctorate in plant genetics from the University of Sussex and works across spaceflight omics, bioinformatics pipelines and open-source environmental sensor hardware.

He is an advocate for open data, including the NASA Open Science Data Repository, and builds interactive educational simulations that bring space biology to the next generation of scientists. At CoSE he advises on what a gravitational-biology lab needs from an instrument and works with customers on experiment design.

Co-founder · Chief Engineer

Jerry Miao

Jerry is a chemist and engineer who led the design of the SciSpinner Max: the printed structure, the circuit boards, the motion firmware and the web interface. He trained in the Gilroy Astrobotany Lab at the University of Wisconsin–Madison and holds a B.S. and an M.S. in Chemistry from UW–Madison.

At CoSE he looks after the engineering side: the workshop in Madison where instruments are built and tested, the open-source control software, and the custom fixtures and instrument variants developed with customer labs.

Co-founder · Education and outreach · now STEMsaic Research Impacts

Travis Tangen

Travis taught high school science for fourteen years before moving into research as broader-impacts lead with WARF at the Wisconsin Institutes for Discovery. He holds an M.S. in Structural Molecular Biology from Birkbeck, University of London, and an M.S. in Science Education from UW–Madison, where he researches STEM partnerships. He now leads STEMsaic Research Impacts, which designs and evaluates research partnerships with universities and colleges.

At CoSE he connected the lab to classrooms, bringing collaborative instrument design and implementation to high school and college programs, and he helped write the benefit-corporation mission. He remains a CoSE supporter. He believes the best research partnerships start when a teacher and a scientist find they’re asking the same question, and grow when students get a real instrument and do the research themselves.

Co-founder · Scientific adviser · Professor of Botany, UW–Madison

Dr. Simon Gilroy

Simon is a plant cell biologist and professor in the Department of Botany at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where he runs the Gilroy Life Sciences Lab. The lab studies, at the cellular level, how plants sense and respond to their environment and how those signals shape development: signal transduction, lipid signalling, tip growth and tropisms. He works extensively with NASA on how plants grow on the International Space Station and on using plants for life support on planetary bases. He holds a Ph.D. from the University of Edinburgh.

Simon co-founded CoSE and was its scientific adviser and one of its funders in the early years, before stepping away from the company. The SciSpinner grew out of his lab, where both Jerry and Richard trained, and his advice on what gravitational-biology experiments need from a clinostat shaped its design.

Founders

How CoSE started

CoSE was founded by Richard Barker, Jerry Miao, Travis Tangen and Simon Gilroy, who met at the Wisconsin Institutes for Discovery, in and around Simon’s astrobotany lab at UW–Madison, and took first place at a WARF Education Start-Up Weekend with a plan to put real research instruments in classrooms. The first product was FlashLapse, a time-lapse imaging system built for summer camps; the two-axis clinostat followed and became the SciSpinner Max.

Travis led the company’s classroom and outreach work and helped write the benefit-corporation mission that remains in the charter; he has since moved on to STEMsaic Research Impacts and remains a supporter.
